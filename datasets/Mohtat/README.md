The Mohtat dataset was segmented using a protocol-aware classification algorithm based on the measured current and voltage profiles. Cycle zero was excluded before processing. Within each remaining cycle, observations with an absolute current not exceeding 2 mA2\ \mathrm{mA}2 mA were classified as relaxation, while active measurements were assigned to charge or discharge according to current polarity. The charging-current sign was assumed to be positive by default, with a heuristic correction for datasets exhibiting a strongly dominant negative-current population.

Constant-voltage phases were detected separately within the active charge and discharge records. A representative constant-current magnitude was estimated as the median of the upper 30% of the active absolute-current distribution. During charging, a measurement was considered a candidate CV observation when its voltage was within 0.01 V0.01\ \mathrm{V}0.01 V of the maximum active charging voltage, its current magnitude was at least 5 mA5\ \mathrm{mA}5 mA below the representative CC magnitude, and its current retained the charging polarity. An analogous criterion was applied during discharge using the minimum active discharge voltage and the discharge-current polarity.

To reduce sensitivity to measurement noise, the absolute-current signal was smoothed using a centered five-sample rolling median. Candidate CV observations were additionally required to exhibit a smoothed current-magnitude gradient not exceeding 1 mA sample−11\ \mathrm{mA\,sample^{-1}}1 mAsample−1, thereby allowing decreasing or approximately stable taper currents while rejecting pronounced current increases. Candidate observations were grouped into contiguous regions, and a region was accepted as a genuine CV phase only if it contained at least 10 samples and had a duration of at least 10 seconds. Active observations not accepted as CV were classified as CC. Short phase fragments containing fewer than five samples were iteratively merged into an adjacent phase, with the longer neighboring region preferred for internal fragments.

Relaxation intervals were associated with the immediately preceding active charge or discharge stage. This produced stage-specific CC, CV, and post-stage relaxation records without imposing an artificial CC-CV structure on every discharge cycle. Consequently, an ordinary discharge could be represented as CC discharge, optionally followed by relaxation, whereas a discharge CV phase was retained only when the voltage-limit, current-reduction, polarity, contiguity, sample-count, and duration criteria were all satisfied. Identical row selections were applied to every measurement channel, preserving sample-wise correspondence among the exported variables. A cycle-level diagnostic file and a dataset-level audit table were generated to document phase counts, inferred protocols, current-sign conventions, discarded cycles, and processing failures.
