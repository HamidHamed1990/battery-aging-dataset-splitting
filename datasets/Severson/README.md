The battery-cycling records were extracted from MATLAB v7.3 batch files and processed independently for each cell and cycle. The time, temperature, charge capacity, discharge capacity, current, and voltage signals were converted to one-dimensional numerical arrays. When the available signals differed in length, they were truncated to the shortest common length to preserve sample-wise alignment. If the time signal was unavailable, the sample index was used as a surrogate temporal coordinate.

Charge and discharge observations were primarily identified from the sign of the measured current. Samples with current greater than a numerical tolerance of 10−810^{-8}10−8 were classified as charging, whereas samples with current below −10−8-10^{-8}−10−8 were classified as discharging. If no informative current signal was available, the algorithm used capacity increments as a fallback criterion. Charge samples were then defined by positive increments in cumulative charge capacity, while discharge samples were defined by positive increments in cumulative discharge capacity. The resulting masks were applied synchronously to the time, temperature, charge-capacity, discharge-capacity, current, and voltage signals.

The extracted charge-current profiles were subsequently partitioned into a sequence of temporally contiguous zones. The first current value whose absolute magnitude did not exceed 0.10.10.1 was used as an initial structural boundary. If present, all preceding observations formed the first zone. Starting from this boundary, additional current plateaus were identified iteratively. For each remaining signal section, the plateau center was estimated as the median of an initialization segment comprising at least 10 samples or 10% of the remaining observations. Plateau variability was characterized by the median absolute deviation, and the acceptance interval was defined using the larger of an absolute tolerance of 0.10.10.1 and three times the median absolute deviation. A plateau was terminated only after five consecutive observations fell outside this interval, thereby reducing sensitivity to isolated current fluctuations. The procedure was repeated on the remaining observations until the complete charge record had been assigned.

Discharge segmentation was based on abrupt changes in the measured current. The absolute first difference of the discharge-current signal was calculated, and observations for which the difference exceeded 0.50.50.5 were identified as current transitions. When one or more transitions were detected, the final transition defined the boundary between two discharge zones. If no transition exceeded the threshold, the complete discharge record was retained as one zone.

A postprocessing stage was used to suppress weakly supported charge zones. When the third zone contained fewer than five samples and a fourth zone was present, the third and fourth zones were concatenated. Optionally, consecutive zones were also merged when neither contained a current plateau. A plateau was considered present when the zone contained at least 20 consecutive observations whose current range did not exceed 0.0050.0050.005. Following each merge, all subsequent zones were renumbered to maintain consecutive zone identifiers. Every detected or merged boundary was applied identically to all measurement channels.
