The cycling data were initially separated into charge and discharge observations using a current-polarity threshold. Measurements with current values greater than 0.1 mA0.1\ \mathrm{mA}0.1 mA were classified as charging, whereas measurements below −0.1 mA-0.1\ \mathrm{mA}−0.1 mA were classified as discharging. Observations within the interval from −0.1-0.1−0.1 to 0.1 mA0.1\ \mathrm{mA}0.1 mA were excluded from both operating regions. A region was retained only when it contained at least three observations.

The extracted charge records were subsequently divided into two temporally ordered zones using a gradient-based transition detector. The discrete numerical gradient of the charge-current signal was first calculated and smoothed using a moving-average filter with a window length of 10 samples. The first index at which the smoothed current gradient decreased below −0.05 mA sample−1-0.05\ \mathrm{mA\,sample^{-1}}−0.05 mAsample−1 was selected as the segmentation boundary. Measurements preceding this index were assigned to the first charge zone, while measurements from the detected index onward were assigned to the second charge zone. If the threshold was not crossed, the complete charge record was assigned to the first zone and the second zone remained empty. The discharge record was not internally segmented and was instead retained in its entirety as a single zone. For every stage, the same segmentation indices were applied to all exported measurement channels, thereby maintaining sample-wise correspondence among current, time, voltage, capacity, and the remaining recorded variables.
