The MATLAB battery records were first converted into an ordered sequence of charge, discharge, and impedance operations. Consecutive duplicate operations were resolved using operation-specific rules: for duplicate charge entries, the second entry was retained, whereas for duplicate discharge or impedance entries, the first entry was retained. Complete electrochemical cycles were subsequently reconstructed through greedy sequence matching. Starting from each charge operation, the algorithm evaluated the admissible protocol patterns in decreasing order of length: charge-impedance-discharge-impedance, charge-impedance-discharge, charge-discharge-impedance, and charge-discharge. The first matching pattern was assigned a new sequential cycle number, and operations that could not be incorporated into one of these patterns were excluded.

The reconstructed charge and discharge stages were segmented independently using the measured current. During charging, the global maximum measured current was used as the reference. Samples within 0.01 A0.01\ \mathrm{A}0.01 A of this maximum were assigned to the first zone, representing the high-current plateau. Samples whose absolute current did not exceed 0.01 A0.01\ \mathrm{A}0.01 A were assigned to a third, near-zero-current zone. All remaining charge samples were assigned to the intermediate second zone. During discharge, samples within 0.01 A0.01\ \mathrm{A}0.01 A of the global minimum current were assigned to the first zone, while samples with an absolute current not exceeding 0.01 A0.01\ \mathrm{A}0.01 A were assigned to the second zone. The first five samples of each charge and discharge record were excluded from the segmented outputs to suppress initialization transients. The resulting sample indices were applied identically to all available time, voltage, current, and temperature channels.

Complex-valued impedance channels were decomposed into their real and imaginary components before impedance-current segmentation. For each impedance record, an initial monotonically increasing current ramp of up to five samples was removed. Using the remaining measurements, the maximum current was selected as the reference level, and samples differing from this reference by less than 5% of its absolute magnitude were identified as stable-current candidates. If several disconnected candidate intervals were present, only the longest contiguous interval was retained as the stable-current zone. All other post-ramp samples were assigned to a second zone. The same zone indices were applied synchronously to every real-valued impedance-related channel having the same number of observations as the battery-current reference.
