The cycling records were processed independently for each battery and cycle using a hierarchical, physics-informed phase-classification procedure. Because the current sign convention was not assumed to be consistent across files, the charging polarity was first inferred from the relationship between current direction and the corresponding voltage increment. For each current polarity, the median one-sample voltage change was calculated using observations whose current magnitude exceeded the rest threshold. The polarity associated with the larger median voltage increase was assigned to charging. When the inference was inconclusive, positive current was adopted as the default charging convention.

Following polarity inference, observations were classified into active charging, active discharging, and relaxation states using a current-magnitude threshold of 0.05 A0.05\ \mathrm{A}0.05 A. Samples whose signed current exceeded 0.05 A0.05\ \mathrm{A}0.05 A were assigned to charging, samples below −0.05 A-0.05\ \mathrm{A}−0.05 A were assigned to discharging, and samples with an absolute current not exceeding 0.05 A0.05\ \mathrm{A}0.05 A were treated as relaxation measurements. Relaxation intervals were associated with the immediately preceding active operating stage, thereby retaining post-charge and post-discharge relaxation periods in the corresponding stage-level records.

Active charging observations were further classified into constant-current and constant-voltage phases. The representative charging-current magnitude was estimated as the median absolute current over all active charging observations. A centered five-sample rolling median was applied to the absolute current signal to reduce sensitivity to isolated measurement fluctuations. A charging observation was identified as a candidate constant-voltage sample when its voltage was within 0.01 V0.01\ \mathrm{V}0.01 V of the maximum charging voltage and its smoothed current magnitude was at least 0.10 A0.10\ \mathrm{A}0.10 A below the representative charging current. Remaining active charging observations were classified as constant-current samples. During discharge, all active observations were classified as constant-current discharge, while near-zero-current observations were classified as relaxation.

To suppress transient classifications caused by measurement noise, contiguous phase runs shorter than three samples were iteratively reassigned to an adjacent phase. For internal short runs, the label of the longer neighboring run was selected, with ties resolved in favor of the preceding run. Short runs at either boundary were assigned the label of their only neighboring region. The detected phases were exported separately while preserving a common set of sample indices across all measurement variables.

The measured test-time coordinate was retained when at least two valid values were available. Otherwise, elapsed time was reconstructed sequentially from absolute timestamps, the data-point counter, or, as a final fallback, the sample index. An audit table records the time source, inferred charge polarity, number of samples assigned to each phase, and consistency of the detected charging protocol with the expected schedule. Constant-voltage charging was expected through cycle 20, but detected phases were never removed solely because they differed from this expectation. Such discrepancies were reported as protocol warnings.
