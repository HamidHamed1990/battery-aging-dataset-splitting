The preprocessing algorithm first reorganizes the cycling measurements into a standardized hierarchy defined by cell, cycle, and operating stage. For each cycle, the discharge and relaxation records are concatenated after translating the relaxation time coordinate such that the first relaxation measurement occurs at a fixed offset after the final discharge measurement. The resulting charge and combined discharge-relaxation records are subsequently segmented using the measured current profile. During charging, the first occurrence of the global maximum current defines the segmentation boundary, whereas during discharging, the boundary is defined by the first occurrence of the global minimum current. Each record is partitioned into two temporally contiguous zones: the first zone contains all observations up to and including the detected extremum, and the second zone contains all subsequent observations. The same boundary is applied synchronously to the voltage, current, capacity, energy, and time channels, thereby preserving alignment among the measured variables.
